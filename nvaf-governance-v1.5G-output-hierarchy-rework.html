NVAF Anticoagulation Risk Assessment Tool

Governance Rationale, Evidence Base, and Intended Use

Author: Dr Prabhath Fernando MBBS MD FRCP
Associated digital tool: NVAF Anticoagulation Risk Assessment Tool (v1.5G OUTPUT HIERARCHY REWORK)
Intended setting: NHS secondary care and community practice
Intended users: Clinicians involved in the management of atrial fibrillation
Version governance marker: GOVERNANCE_MARKER_NVAF_V1_5G_OUTPUT_HIERARCHY_REWORK

1. Purpose and Scope

This document describes the clinical rationale, evidence base, governance considerations, and intended use of the NVAF Anticoagulation Risk Assessment Tool (v1.5G OUTPUT HIERARCHY REWORK).

The tool is designed to support clinicians in balancing stroke prevention and bleeding risk when considering oral anticoagulation (OAC) in patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF), particularly in older adults and those with frailty, multimorbidity, or competing risks.1–3

The current output-hierarchy build:

  • Uses validated, guideline-endorsed risk scores (CHA2DS2-VASc and ORBIT) without modification.4–6
  • Presents a revised visible hierarchy of overall interpretation, clinical balance, annual risk comparison, threshold position, and frailty context.
  • Provides drug-specific modelled annual risk translation for warfarin and selected DOAC regimens.8–11
  • Includes treated intracranial haemorrhage estimates for the selected anticoagulant within the scientific detail layer.8–11
  • Retains frailty as a qualitative interpretation modifier only; frailty does not numerically alter the risk scores or modelled annual estimates.
  • Presents a calmer first-read output structure for discussion support, with expanded clinician-facing numerical detail available separately.
  • Includes annualised NNT and NNH estimates in the scientific detail layer, derived from annual absolute risk differences.
  • Separates published ORBIT observed anticoagulated bleeding context from calibrated treated-bleeding warfarin anchors used for risk translation.
Important: This tool does not replace clinical judgement, specialist advice, shared decision-making, or local/national guidance. It is designed to make clinical reasoning transparent and auditable, not to mandate a treatment decision.

2. Clinical Background

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is a major risk factor for ischaemic stroke, with AF-related strokes associated with higher mortality and greater long-term disability than non-AF strokes. Oral anticoagulation substantially reduces stroke risk in NVAF but carries an inherent risk of bleeding, including major bleeding and intracranial haemorrhage.1–3,8–12

Clinical decision-making in AF therefore requires balancing:

  • Absolute stroke risk without anticoagulation, and
  • Absolute bleeding risk with anticoagulation.

The current build also displays an estimated annual major bleeding risk without anticoagulation. This estimate is included to support balanced clinical discussion and to avoid the misleading impression that patients not receiving anticoagulation have no bleeding risk. However, that untreated bleeding estimate is a modelled anchor derived from observational non-anticoagulated AF cohorts and should not be interpreted as an individual prediction.7

The current build additionally displays a treated annual estimate for intracranial haemorrhage with the selected anticoagulant. This value is intended to reflect a clinically important harm signal that many clinicians consider separately from overall major bleeding. However, intracranial haemorrhage without anticoagulation is not yet separately modelled in this build, and therefore the tool does not yet calculate an ICH-specific absolute difference or ICH-specific NNH.

This balance is particularly challenging in:

  • Older adults
  • Patients with frailty
  • Those with renal impairment, anaemia, or prior bleeding
  • Patients with competing risks, limited physiological reserve, or limited life expectancy

Guidelines consistently recommend assessing both stroke and bleeding risk, but also emphasise that bleeding risk scores should be used to identify modifiable risk factors, not as sole determinants to withhold anticoagulation.2,3,12

The NVAF Anticoagulation Risk Assessment Tool was developed to make this reasoning explicit, structured, and auditable, rather than implicit or inconsistent.

Representation of older and frail patients in clinical trials

Although oral anticoagulation is supported by robust evidence demonstrating stroke risk reduction in NVAF, it is important to recognise that older adults and patients with frailty have been under-represented in many landmark anticoagulation trials.8–11

Pivotal randomised controlled trials of warfarin and direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) predominantly enrolled relatively fit older adults, with limited inclusion of patients with advanced frailty, multimorbidity, cognitive impairment, high falls risk, or limited life expectancy. As a result, trial populations may not fully reflect the complexity of patients encountered in routine clinical practice, particularly within secondary care and geriatric medicine settings.

Consequently, estimates of absolute stroke reduction and bleeding risk derived from these trials represent population averages, and their direct applicability to frail individuals requires careful clinical interpretation. In frailty, the consequences of both stroke and major bleeding may be amplified, while competing risks and patient priorities may differ substantially from those represented in trial cohorts.

This recognised evidence gap underlines the importance of individualised decision-making, shared discussion with patients and carers, and cautious application of guideline recommendations in older and frail populations. The NVAF tool is designed to support this nuanced judgement rather than to extrapolate trial findings uncritically to all patients.

3. Stroke Risk Assessment: CHA₂DS₂-VASc

The tool uses the CHA2DS2-VASc score to estimate typical annual stroke risk in patients with NVAF who are not anticoagulated.4,5

CHA2DS2-VASc is:

  • Widely validated across diverse populations4,5
  • Recommended by major international guidelines2,3
  • Simple to apply in routine clinical practice

Stroke risk estimates presented in the tool are anchored to the single monotonic CHA₂DS₂-VASc annual stroke-risk table used in the current build: 0.66, 1.45, 2.92, 4.28, 6.46, 9.97, 12.52, 13.96, 14.10, and 15.89 per 100 patient-years for scores 0 to 9 respectively. These values are used here as discussion-support anchors rather than as precise individual predictions.

In keeping with guideline recommendations:

  • CHA2DS2-VASc is used without modification.
  • Sex-specific treatment thresholds are respected.2,3
  • Baseline untreated stroke risk, derived from the monotonic annual risk table used in this build, is the stroke anchor for subsequent treated-risk modelling.

4. Bleeding Assessment: ORBIT and the Untreated Bleeding Anchor

4.1 Rationale for selecting ORBIT

Several bleeding risk scores exist, including HAS-BLED, ATRIA, and ORBIT. The present tool uses ORBIT for the following reasons:

Predictive performance
ORBIT has been shown to perform at least as well as, and in some studies better than, HAS-BLED for predicting major bleeding in anticoagulated AF patients.6

Objective variables
ORBIT relies predominantly on objective clinical variables (age, renal function, haemoglobin/anaemia, prior bleeding, antiplatelet therapy), reducing subjectivity and inter-observer variability.6

Simplicity and clinical usability
ORBIT avoids labile INR and alcohol-use variables, making it particularly suitable in the era of DOACs and in settings where warfarin-specific metrics are less relevant.6,12

Alignment with contemporary practice
ORBIT was developed and validated in cohorts including patients treated with both warfarin and direct oral anticoagulants, making it applicable to modern anticoagulation strategies.6

For these reasons, ORBIT was selected as the bleeding-vulnerability framework in this tool while recognising that no bleeding score perfectly predicts individual outcomes.

4.2 Important clarification: ORBIT is not used to derive untreated baseline bleeding risk directly

ORBIT predicts major bleeding risk in anticoagulated AF populations. It therefore cannot be used directly as a validated predictor of baseline major bleeding risk in untreated NVAF.6

In the current build, ORBIT is used for three purposes:

  • To display anticoagulated bleeding-vulnerability context
  • To support bleeding-risk mitigation and clinical discussion
  • To stratify the observational untreated bleeding anchor into low, medium, and high vulnerability categories

4.3 Untreated major bleeding anchor used in the current build

To support balanced side-by-side display of risk without anticoagulation and with selected anticoagulant, the current build uses an observational untreated major bleeding anchor, stratified by ORBIT category.7

  • ORBIT 0–2: 0.4 per 100 patient-years
  • ORBIT 3: 0.8 per 100 patient-years
  • ORBIT ≥4: 1.5 per 100 patient-years

These values are intended as evidence-bounded, discussion-support anchors rather than direct patient-specific predictions. They were chosen to sit within the range reported in observational studies of AF patients not receiving anticoagulation.7

Governance note:
Baseline major bleeding without anticoagulation in the current build is not derived by scaling down ORBIT anticoagulated bleeding percentages. It is anchored to observational non-anticoagulated AF cohorts and stratified by ORBIT category to reflect bleeding vulnerability.6,7

5. Drug-specific Annual Risk Translation Model

The principal feature of the current build remains a drug-specific annual risk translation layer.

The model structure is:

  1. Estimate baseline annual stroke risk from CHA2DS2-VASc.
  2. Estimate baseline annual major bleeding risk without anticoagulation using the observational untreated bleeding anchor stratified by ORBIT category.6,7
  3. Apply drug-specific relative effects to derive treated annual stroke and treated annual major bleeding estimates.8–11
  4. Display a treated annual intracranial haemorrhage estimate for the selected anticoagulant, based on trial-anchored evidence for that drug.8–11

The current build supports:

  • Warfarin (INR 2–3)
  • Apixaban 5 mg twice daily
  • Apixaban 2.5 mg twice daily
  • Dabigatran 150 mg twice daily
  • Dabigatran 110 mg twice daily
  • Rivaroxaban
  • Edoxaban 60 mg
  • Edoxaban 30 mg

These treated-risk estimates are modelled annual estimates derived from the baseline anchor plus therapy-specific relative effects. They are intended to support comparison and discussion, not to function as a patient-specific predictive model.8–11

The intracranial haemorrhage estimate is displayed as an annual treated-risk figure only. In the current build it is not yet linked to a separately modelled untreated ICH anchor, and therefore does not yet generate an ICH-specific absolute difference or ICH-specific NNH.

5.1 Bleeding model calibration in the current build

The original ORBIT derivation study reported observed major bleeding rates of approximately 2.4, 4.7 and 8.1 events per 100 patient-years across low-, medium- and high-risk strata.6 In the current build, these published ORBIT stratum rates are retained as observed anticoagulated bleeding context for the selected ORBIT category.

However, these published ORBIT rates are not drug-specific treated bleeding estimates. When used directly as treated warfarin anchors and then combined with drug-specific hazard ratios versus warfarin from pivotal anticoagulant trials, the resulting displayed annual treated bleeding estimates appeared excessively pessimistic in scenario testing, particularly for DOACs with more favourable bleeding profiles.

The current build therefore separates:

  • Published ORBIT observed anticoagulated bleeding context: low approximately 2.5%, medium 4.7%, high 8.1% per year
  • Calibrated treated-bleeding warfarin anchors used for model translation: low 2.0%, medium 3.5%, high 5.0% per year

These calibrated treated-bleeding anchors are modelling values, not direct cohort-derived event rates. They were selected pragmatically after scenario testing in order to:

  • Preserve ordinal bleeding-risk stratification across ORBIT bands
  • Preserve drug-relative bleeding differences
  • Avoid excessively pessimistic treated annual bleeding outputs
  • Improve bedside plausibility of displayed estimates

Treated annual bleeding estimates for individual anticoagulants are then generated by applying drug-specific relative effects versus warfarin to the calibrated treated-bleeding warfarin anchor for the relevant ORBIT category.

Worked example: high ORBIT calibration
For the high-ORBIT category, the calibrated treated-bleeding warfarin anchor is set at 5.0% / year rather than directly using the published ORBIT high-risk observed rate of approximately 8.1% / year as a drug-translation anchor.
  • Apixaban: 5.0 × 0.69 = 3.5% / year
  • Dabigatran 150 mg: 5.0 × 0.93 = 4.7% / year
  • Dabigatran 110 mg: 5.0 × 0.80 = 4.0% / year
  • Rivaroxaban: 5.0 × 1.04 = 5.2% / year

These values remain high, consistent with significant bleeding vulnerability, but were judged more clinically plausible for bedside decision support than direct translation from the original ORBIT high-risk observed rate.

Governance interpretation:
The calibrated treated-bleeding anchors in the current build are transparent modelling assumptions introduced to improve bedside plausibility. They should be interpreted as calibration values for comparative discussion support, not as precise individual predictions of bleeding risk.

5.2 Why the model does not produce a single net-benefit score

The tool still does not calculate a single numerical “net benefit” score. There is currently no validated, guideline-endorsed method for combining stroke and bleeding risk scores into a single predictive metric for individual patients that adequately accounts for frailty, competing mortality, treatment burden, and patient priorities.2,3,12

Instead, the tool provides:

  • A calmer first-read comparison of annual risks
  • A short clinical balance statement
  • An explicit threshold-position summary
  • Optional expanded scientific detail for clinicians

6. Design Principles and Safety Features

The NVAF tool was developed using explicit safety-by-design principles appropriate for clinical decision support.

6.1 Use of validated scores without modification

CHA2DS2-VASc and ORBIT are used unchanged, preserving their validated structure, thresholds, and interpretation.4–6

6.2 Calm visible output architecture

The visible interface is intentionally restrained. The main screen presents:

  • Overall interpretation
  • Clinical balance
  • Annual risk comparison
  • Threshold position
  • Frailty context

More detailed numerical material is placed in an expandable scientific detail section to minimise cognitive overload during clinical discussion.

6.3 Structured transparency in the scientific detail layer

The expanded scientific detail section provides:

  • Evidence summary
  • Extrapolation / modelling assumptions
  • Interpretation notes
  • Model inputs and outputs
  • Absolute effect estimates
  • Treated intracranial haemorrhage estimate for the selected anticoagulant

6.4 NNT and NNH

In the current build, the expanded scientific section includes:

  • NNT over 1 year for stroke reduction
  • NNH over 1 year for major bleeding increase

These are derived from annual absolute risk differences rather than from lifetime or multi-year projections. They are intended as discussion-support metrics and should be interpreted in the context of frailty, competing risks, baseline prognosis, and patient priorities.

NNT / NNH in the current build are derived annualised estimates.
They are not individual predictions and do not account for competing mortality, adherence, treatment discontinuation, or changes in clinical context over time.

6.5 Intracranial bleeding

The current build displays a treated intracranial haemorrhage estimate for the selected anticoagulant within the scientific detail section.8–11

However, intracranial haemorrhage without anticoagulation is still not separately modelled in this build. For that reason:

  • No untreated ICH comparator is displayed
  • No ICH-specific absolute difference is calculated
  • No ICH-specific NNH is currently generated

This means that NNH in the present build still relates to major bleeding, not specifically to intracranial haemorrhage.

6.6 Decision context

Decision context does NOT mean “confidence in the tool”.
It is not a measure of tool reliability and not a rating of the evidence base. It describes what primarily determines the recommendation in that scenario: guideline-concordant threshold logic, patient preferences, or prognosis/goals-of-care.

6.7 Frailty handling: qualitative modifier with explicit safeguards

Frailty is not a validated quantitative modifier of CHA2DS2-VASc or ORBIT. The tool therefore does not adjust numeric risk scores or modelled annual estimates based on CFS. Instead, CFS is used as a contextual layer that changes the interpretation language and the decision-context framing.

Frailty tiers used operationally in the current build:

  • CFS 1–4: no frailty-driven numeric change
  • CFS 5–6: greater emphasis on vulnerability, recovery, and priorities
  • CFS 7: preference-sensitive framing
  • CFS 8–9: prognosis-dominant / individualised care planning framing
Safety safeguard in very severe frailty / terminal illness:
The tool is deliberately designed so that very severe frailty or terminal illness shifts interpretation toward prognosis, competing risks, time-to-benefit, and goals-of-care. Frailty does not numerically lower stroke risk or raise bleeding risk within the model; it changes the way modelled estimates should be interpreted.

6.8 Internal consistency checks

To prevent contradictory inputs:

  • Age ≥75 years is defined once within CHA2DS2-VASc and derived automatically for ORBIT.

This prevents logically inconsistent age-band selection across risk scores and reduces user error.

6.9 Reset functionality

A reset function is included to:

  • Clear all inputs between assessments
  • Reduce risk of cross-patient data contamination
  • Support safe use in busy clinical environments

7. Interpretation and Clinical Use

The tool is intended to be used as part of a structured clinical assessment, not as a standalone decision-maker.

Clinicians should:

  • Use the outputs to inform discussion with patients and carers
  • Consider cognition, falls risk, comorbidity, functional reserve, and life expectancy
  • Address modifiable bleeding risk factors where possible
  • Document the reasoning behind anticoagulation decisions, particularly when the decision context is preference-sensitive or prognosis-dominant

The tool may be particularly helpful in:

  • Older adults with intermediate or threshold-zone stroke risk
  • Patients with prior bleeding, anaemia, CKD, or antiplatelet use
  • Situations where clinicians wish to compare specific anticoagulant options in a transparent way
  • Situations where patients or carers benefit from plain-language annualised risk translation
  • Situations where clinicians wish to consider major bleeding and intracranial haemorrhage as related but distinct harms

8. Limitations

This tool has several important limitations:

  • Risk estimates are derived from population-level data and do not predict individual outcomes.
  • The untreated major bleeding anchor is a modelled observational anchor, not a validated patient-specific untreated-bleeding score.7
  • Published ORBIT anticoagulated bleeding rates are observational category-level context measures, not drug-specific treated-risk estimates.6
  • Calibrated treated-bleeding warfarin anchors are transparent modelling values selected after scenario testing and are not direct cohort-derived event rates.
  • Drug-specific treated risks are modelled annual estimates and may not fully account for cross-trial differences in case mix.8–11
  • Treated intracranial haemorrhage estimates are trial-anchored and should be interpreted cautiously across trials.8–11
  • Untreated intracranial haemorrhage is not yet separately modelled.
  • Frailty is heterogeneous and not fully captured by existing risk scores.
  • Competing risks and non-cardiovascular mortality are not numerically modelled.
  • The tool does not replace specialist input in complex cases.

These limitations are inherent to current evidence and are explicitly acknowledged within the tool’s design.

9. Digital Clinical Safety and Governance Considerations

The NVAF tool is a clinical decision support tool. It does not automate a treatment decision and is not intended to function as a medical device that mandates therapy.

It is intended to:

  • Reduce unwarranted variation by making reasoning explicit
  • Promote transparent documentation and shared decision-making
  • Support governance review and clinician education

Version control is maintained. Substantive changes should be driven by evidence or guideline updates. Changes to modelling assumptions or output framing should be explicitly documented so that the tool logic, visible interface, scientific detail, and governance text remain aligned.

Governance principle for ongoing development:
The visible interface, modelling layer, and governance document should be updated together. Silent logic drift between tool outputs and governance wording should be avoided.

10. Conclusion

The NVAF Anticoagulation Risk Assessment Tool (v1.5G OUTPUT HIERARCHY REWORK) supports clinicians in navigating the balance between stroke prevention and bleeding risk, particularly in older adults and patients with frailty where decision-making is often nuanced.

By combining validated, guideline-endorsed risk scores (CHA2DS2-VASc and ORBIT) without modification, adding a transparent drug-specific annual risk translation model, retaining treated intracranial haemorrhage as a separate clinically relevant harm signal within the scientific detail layer, and explicitly documenting calibrated treated-bleeding anchors as modelling assumptions, the tool promotes structured clinical reasoning while trying to avoid false precision.4–11

The current build is intended to support, not replace, clinical judgement, shared decision-making, and appropriate documentation. Used within appropriate clinical governance frameworks, it may help reduce unwarranted variation, enhance transparency of decision-making, and improve the quality of clinician–patient discussions regarding anticoagulation in NVAF.
