ISS-Lite (Pragmatic) Trauma Severity Calculator

Governance, Methods & Evidence (v1.2 — locked; aligns with Tool v1.5 spine-separated grouped behaviour)

Purpose

ISS-Lite is a clinician-facing bedside decision-support aid intended to help structure thinking about overall injury burden using simplified severity bands (0–5). It is designed to support education, structured communication, and escalation decisions in real-time clinical settings.

Critical governance statement: ISS-Lite is not formal Injury Severity Score (ISS) and does not constitute Abbreviated Injury Scale (AIS) coding. It must not be used for trauma registry submission, audit reporting, contractual metrics, or as a substitute for specialist clinical judgement.

Scope and intended users

  • Intended users: clinicians involved in acute trauma assessment and inpatient trauma care (ED, acute medicine, trauma teams, orthopaedics/orthogeriatrics).
  • Use setting: bedside assessment, handover, MDT communication, and escalation prompting.
  • Not intended for: NMTR/registry submission, AIS/ISS coding workflows, benchmarking, or research datasets without separate validation.

How the tool works

User interface (head-to-toe layout)

For usability, the tool presents body regions in a head-to-toe sequence and includes separate spine selections: Head/Brain, Face, Cervical spine, Chest, Thoracic spine, Abdomen/Pelvic contents, Lumbar spine, Extremities/Pelvic girdle, External.

Core method (ISS-style mathematics, without claiming AIS coding)

The tool uses clinician-selected severity bands (0–5). It then squares and sums the three highest regional severities: ISS-Lite = a² + b² + c².

This mirrors the mathematical structure of ISS but does not claim equivalence, because ISS-Lite uses pragmatic bedside banding rather than formal AIS-coded injuries.

Six-region behaviour retained (not 9)

ISS-Lite still behaves like a 6-region system, not 9. The additional spine dropdowns are present to improve usability and reduce misclassification risk (i.e., “where do I put this spinal injury?”).

Important governance note (verbatim):
Spinal regions are separated for usability. For ISS-Lite calculation, cervical spine is grouped with Head/Neck, and thoracic/lumbar spine are grouped with Extremities, consistent with AIS regional principles. The highest severity within each grouped region is used.

Grouping rule used by the calculator

  • Head/Neck group = max(Head/Brain, Cervical spine)
  • Extremities group = max(Extremities/Pelvic girdle, Thoracic spine, Lumbar spine)
  • Other regions are unchanged: Face, Chest, Abdomen/Pelvic contents, External

The ISS-Lite calculation then takes the top three from these six grouped regions, squares them, and sums them.

Severity banding (0–5) and uncertainty handling

Each region contains embedded pragmatic guidance for selecting bands 0–5, including examples. These descriptors are designed to be conservative and to support consistency, but are not presented as a formal mapping to AIS.

Uncertainty rule: if uncertain between two categories, users are advised to select the higher band or escalate for formal review. This is an intentional safety feature to reduce under-triage.

Interpretation prompts

These prompts are conservative and intended to support escalation thinking only.

  • ISS-Lite ≥16: “Likely major trauma” escalation prompt.
  • ISS-Lite 9–15: moderate injury burden; lower threshold to escalate if uncertainty or clinical concern.
  • ISS-Lite <9: lower injury burden; does not exclude major risk from physiology, frailty, or anticoagulation.

Explicit limitations and exclusions (read carefully)

  • ISS-Lite is not AIS-coded and has not been validated against formal ISS.
  • Must not be used for NMTR/registry submission, audit reporting, contractual metrics, or coding purposes.
  • Does not incorporate physiology (e.g., GCS, BP, lactate), airway threat, shock, mechanism, or time course.
  • Does not adjust for age, frailty, anticoagulation/antiplatelets, comorbidity, or competing mortality risk.
  • Does not replace imaging review, specialist interpretation, or evolving clinical reassessment.
  • Embedded examples are pragmatic and intentionally conservative; they may not match local registry coding in edge cases.
Knowledge gap acknowledgement:
There is currently no validated, widely adopted bedside substitute for formal AIS/ISS coding. ISS-Lite is offered transparently as a pragmatic guide, not a surrogate standard.

Governance and safe use principles

  • Use as a structured communication tool (e.g., record the top contributors and severity bands) and an escalation aid.
  • Escalate where band selection is uncertain, discordant with clinical concern, or physiology is abnormal.
  • Local governance approval is recommended prior to deployment beyond education/teaching use.
  • Any local adaptation must preserve attribution, disclaimers, and version control markers.

Evidence and references (for verification and scrutiny)

References are provided to allow verification of the canonical ISS/AIS concepts and to support transparent scrutiny. Their inclusion does not imply that ISS-Lite reproduces AIS coding or registry definitions.

  1. Baker SP, O’Neill B, Haddon W, Long WB. The Injury Severity Score: a method for describing patients with multiple injuries and evaluating emergency care. Journal of Trauma. 1974;14:187–196.
  2. Association for the Advancement of Automotive Medicine (AAAM). Abbreviated Injury Scale (AIS) 2005 – Update 2008. Barrington, IL: AAAM.
  3. National Major Trauma Registry (NMTR), UK. Injury inclusion criteria and coding methodology (current national specifications).
