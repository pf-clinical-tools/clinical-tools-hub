1. Introduction

Heart failure is a clinical syndrome. In patients with preserved (≥50%) or mildly reduced (41–49%) EF, diagnosis is frequently probabilistic and requires integration of symptoms/signs, natriuretic peptides, and imaging findings.1 Echocardiographic “diastolic” indices and structural surrogates can be informative but are context-dependent, may be neutral at rest, and are often limited by rhythm, valve disease, and technical quality.2–4

This tool supports structured clinical documentation in Acute Medicine, Geriatrics, and General Internal Medicine by combining:

  • a clinical syndrome gate,
  • a strict EF scope gate,
  • structural echocardiographic supportive features (when available), and
  • NICE-aligned natriuretic peptide interpretation.1

It deliberately avoids definitive diagnostic labels and avoids diastolic grading.

2. Purpose and intended use

  • Support clinical interpretation and documentation of diastolic-relevant echo context alongside natriuretic peptides in suspected HF.
  • Provide copy-ready text for clinical notes/letters summarising probabilistic interpretation.
  • Apply safety-first gates to prevent out-of-scope use (including EF ≤40% stop rule).
  • Make explicit when interpretation is limited by rhythm/valve disease/technical quality.

Scope boundaries

  • Applies to suspected HF with EF ≥41% (preserved or mildly reduced).
  • Does not replace formal echocardiography reporting.
  • Does not assign diastolic grade or filling pressures.
  • Does not independently establish HFpEF diagnosis.
  • Does not mandate treatment; decisions remain clinician-led.

3. Geriatric multimorbidity context (why this tool exists)

In older adults, breathlessness, reduced exercise tolerance, and oedema are frequently multifactorial. Frailty/deconditioning may be a dominant contributor to symptoms (particularly when activity levels fall), and comorbid conditions (anaemia, obesity, chronic lung disease, chest wall restriction) can materially influence both symptom burden and test interpretation.

This tool therefore:

  • requires a clinical syndrome gate confirming HF physiology is a meaningful contributor before any output is generated;
  • treats comorbid contributors as interpretive modifiers and safety prompts (wording), not as “threshold adjusters”;
  • keeps output probabilistic and documentation-focused.

Importantly, asymptomatic diastolic dysfunction is common, and treatment is not directed at “diastolic dysfunction” in isolation. The practical task in acute/general medicine is often estimating the contribution of HF-type physiology to symptoms and congestion.

4. Scope gates and safety controls

4.1 Module 1 — Clinical HF syndrome gate

User must confirm both:

  • Symptoms/signs consistent with HF syndrome, and
  • HF physiology is considered a likely/significant contributor (even if co-contributors exist).

4.2 EF gate — scope control

  • EF ≥50% or 41–49% → in scope
  • EF ≤40% → STOP (tool does not apply)

5. Echocardiographic interpretability and valve-dominant safeguards

The tool never assigns diastolic grade. It records interpretability flags (AF, significant MR, moderate/severe AS, limited Doppler quality, pacing/fusion) and generates explicit wording when these limitations are present.

5.1 Valve-dominant pathways

Significant MR or moderate/severe AS triggers a valve-dominant output pathway:

  • HFpEF attribution is treated as indeterminate within the framework,
  • dual pathology (valve disease plus HFpEF) is acknowledged as possible,
  • referral prompts are provided in the treatment/actions block.

6. Structural echo supportive features and optional Doppler context

6.1 Inputs and thresholds

Structural supportive context

  • LA support: LAVI >34 ml/m² OR qualitative LA enlargement
  • LV wall thickness support: IVS ≥11 mm or PWT ≥11 mm
  • Pulmonary pressure support: TR Vmax >2.8 m/s OR PASP >35 mmHg

Optional Doppler context (documentation; supportive only when interpretable)

The tool may record Doppler parameters (e′ velocity, E/e′, E/A ratio, deceleration time) to support structured clinical interpretation and documentation. These parameters are not used to assign diastolic grade and do not independently establish a HFpEF phenotype within this framework.

Where rhythm, significant valve disease, pacing/fusion, or technical limitation is present, Doppler parameters are recorded for completeness but are not used for HFpEF attribution within this framework.

When Doppler parameters are interpretable (sinus rhythm, no significant MR, no moderate/severe AS, adequate image quality, no pacing/fusion), abnormal findings (for example raised E/e′ or reduced e′ velocities) may provide supportive evidence of elevated filling pressures within the overall clinical context. However, Doppler findings do not independently establish HFpEF, do not override low natriuretic peptide values, and do not supersede valve-dominant safeguards.2,4

7. Natriuretic peptides module

Thresholds (NICE):

  • NT-proBNP: <400 low; 400–2000 intermediate; ≥2000 high
  • BNP: <100 low; 100–399 intermediate; ≥400 high

Safety rules

  • If NP tier is low, tool states HF is less likely and echo surrogates should not override low NP within this framework.
  • If NP is unknown (not entered), tool does not output “supportive HFpEF” language or SGLT2 consideration text.

8. Outputs, actions, and referral prompts

The tool produces a copyable “Interpretive Documentation” block and a separate non-copied “Treatment/Actions” block. Actions are framed as context-dependent prompts, not directives.

8.1 Referral prompts (non-directive)

The tool suggests considering cardiology referral where any of the following apply:

  • moderate/severe AS or significant MR with symptoms, recurrent admissions, or uncertainty about severity/symptom attribution,
  • discordance (high NP but neutral resting echo supportive features),
  • recurrent admissions or persistent diagnostic uncertainty,
  • suspected pulmonary hypertension/RV dysfunction, or significant arrhythmia burden.

8.2 SGLT2 text condition

The tool displays SGLT2 consideration text only when EF is ≥50 or 41–49, NP tier is high, structural support is present, and moderate/severe AS is not present. In frail older adults or those with poor intake, the tool emphasises stability, hydration risk, and monitoring.

9. Limitations

  • No diastolic grading or filling pressure estimation is performed.2,4
  • Resting echo may be neutral in exertional/load-dependent HFpEF.
  • AF and dominant valve disease materially limit attribution; tool defaults to “indeterminate” for HFpEF attribution in these contexts.
  • Comorbidity/frailty modifiers are not used to “adjust thresholds”; they are used for safety wording only.

10. Governance, safety, and version control

The live tool implementation (v1.2.5 LOCKED) is treated as authoritative.

Future modifications require explicit version increment, change log, and governance review for logic drift.

11. Disclaimer

This is a clinical documentation support tool. It does not replace clinician judgement, formal echo reporting, specialist cardiology input, or invasive assessment where indicated. Responsibility for clinical decisions remains with the treating clinician.
