ISS-Lite (Pragmatic) Trauma Severity Calculator

Governance, Methods & Evidence (v1.3A — locked; aligns with Tool v1.6)

Purpose

ISS-Lite is a clinician-facing bedside decision-support aid intended to help structure thinking about overall injury burden using simplified severity bands (0–5). It is designed to support education, structured communication, and escalation prompting (and structured escalation discussion) in real-time clinical settings.

Critical governance statement: ISS-Lite is not formal Injury Severity Score (ISS) and does not constitute Abbreviated Injury Scale (AIS) coding. It must not be used for trauma registry submission, audit reporting, contractual metrics, or as a substitute for specialist clinical judgement.

Scope and intended users

  • Intended users: clinicians involved in acute trauma assessment and inpatient trauma care (ED, acute medicine, trauma teams, orthopaedics/orthogeriatrics).
  • Use setting: bedside assessment, handover, MDT communication, and escalation prompting.
  • Not intended for: NMTR/registry submission, AIS/ISS coding workflows, benchmarking, or research datasets without separate validation.

How the tool works

User interface (head-to-toe layout)

For usability, the tool presents body regions in a head-to-toe sequence and separates spinal regions: Head/Brain, Face, Cervical spine, Chest, Thoracic spine, Abdomen/Pelvic contents, Lumbar spine, Extremities/Pelvic girdle, External.

Core method (ISS-style mathematics, without claiming AIS coding)

ISS-Lite uses clinician-selected severity bands (0–5) as a pragmatic proxy for anatomical injury burden. After grouping back to six canonical regions, it identifies the three highest regional severities and applies the ISS-style calculation: ISS-Lite = a² + b² + c².

Why use the top three, squared? Canonical ISS summarises multi-injury anatomical burden by taking the three most severely injured body regions. This design reflects the practical reality that overall risk and resource need are usually driven by the most severe injuries, while minor additional injuries contribute relatively less. Squaring the severity values means that increasing severity has a disproportionately larger contribution to the total score, reducing false equivalence between different injury patterns. ISS-Lite mirrors this mathematical structure (top-three selection and squared-sum) but does not claim equivalence, because ISS-Lite uses pragmatic bedside severity bands rather than formal AIS-coded injuries and does not generate registry-valid ISS.

ISS structural alignment statement:
ISS-Lite aligns with the canonical mathematical structure of the Injury Severity Score (ISS), as originally described by Baker et al. and operationalised within AIS-based trauma scoring frameworks. Specifically, it applies the six anatomical region model and calculates the squared sum of the three highest regional severities (a² + b² + c²).

However, ISS-Lite does not perform formal Abbreviated Injury Scale (AIS) coding, does not assign AIS dictionary codes, and does not generate registry-valid ISS values. Its alignment is structural (regional grouping and mathematical method) rather than definitional (AIS injury coding or NMTR registry processes).

Six-region behaviour retained (not 9)

Canonical ISS is built on six anatomical regions (Head/Neck, Face, Chest, Abdomen/Pelvic contents, Extremities/Pelvic girdle, External). ISS-Lite intentionally retains this six-region behaviour when calculating the severity score, even though the bedside interface divides the body into nine selectable regions to improve clarity and reduce misclassification (particularly for spinal injuries).

The additional spine dropdowns are present for bedside usability and to reduce misclassification risk (i.e., “where do I put this spinal injury?”). They do not create additional independent scoring regions. Instead, spine selections are grouped back into the canonical six-region structure before the top-three values are selected and summed.

Important governance note (verbatim):
Spinal regions are separated for usability. For ISS-Lite calculation, cervical spine is grouped with Head/Neck, and thoracic/lumbar spine are grouped with Extremities, conceptually consistent with AIS regional grouping principles. The highest severity within each grouped region is used.

Grouping rule used by the calculator

  • Head/Neck group = max(Head/Brain, Cervical spine)
  • Extremities group = max(Extremities/Pelvic girdle, Thoracic spine, Lumbar spine)
  • Other regions are unchanged: Face, Chest, Abdomen/Pelvic contents, External

The ISS-Lite calculation then takes the top three from these six grouped regions, squares them, and sums them.

Severity banding (0–5) and uncertainty handling

Each region uses a pragmatic clinician-selected severity band from 0 to 5. These bands are supported by embedded examples intended to improve consistency in bedside use. They are deliberately conservative and designed to reflect increasing anatomical injury burden (rather than physiological derangement), but they are not presented as formal mappings to AIS codes.

Why severity is limited to 0–5 (and excludes 6)

In formal Abbreviated Injury Scale (AIS) methodology, severity ranges from 1 to 6, where AIS 6 represents “maximal / currently untreatable” injury. In canonical ISS calculation, the presence of any AIS 6 injury automatically results in ISS = 75.

ISS-Lite deliberately excludes a severity level of 6. This is an intentional governance boundary and not an omission.

Rationale for excluding severity 6:
  • ISS-Lite does not perform formal AIS coding.
  • Determination of “unsurvivable” injury requires specialist coding expertise and often retrospective registry validation.
  • Bedside clinicians should avoid premature assignment of futility based solely on anatomical estimation.
  • Including a “6” category could imply equivalence with AIS methodology, which ISS-Lite explicitly does not claim.

Injuries that might correspond to AIS 6 in formal coding should be captured within band 5 (critical) in ISS-Lite and managed according to physiology, trajectory, and specialist trauma input.

Mathematical implications

Because ISS-Lite uses a maximum band of 5, the highest possible score is: 5² + 5² + 5² = 75.

This preserves the canonical maximum value seen in formal ISS, while maintaining a clear methodological separation from AIS-coded injury scoring.

Uncertainty handling rule

Safety principle: If uncertain between two categories, users are advised to select the higher band or escalate for senior or specialist review. This conservative bias is an intentional safeguard to reduce under-triage risk.

Severity selection should always be interpreted alongside physiology, mechanism, frailty, anticoagulation status, and clinical trajectory. ISS-Lite does not replace ongoing reassessment.

Interpretation prompts

These prompts are conservative and intended to support escalation thinking only.

  • ISS-Lite ≥16: escalation prompt consistent with commonly used “major trauma” thresholds in registry literature; does not substitute for local trauma team activation criteria.
  • ISS-Lite 9–15: moderate injury burden; lower threshold to escalate if uncertainty or clinical concern.
  • ISS-Lite <9: lower injury burden; does not exclude major risk from physiology, frailty, or anticoagulation.

These thresholds are heuristic prompts to support structured escalation discussion; they do not replace local trauma activation criteria, senior clinical judgement, or evolving clinical reassessment.

Explicit limitations and exclusions (read carefully)

  • ISS-Lite is not AIS-coded and has not been validated against formal ISS.
  • ISS-Lite values must not be numerically equated with registry-calculated ISS values, even when similar numbers are produced.
  • ISS-Lite has not been prospectively validated against formal ISS, mortality, or outcome prediction models.
  • ISS-Lite is not a mortality prediction tool and should not be used to estimate probability of survival.
  • Must not be used for NMTR/registry submission, audit reporting, contractual metrics, or coding purposes.
  • Does not incorporate physiology (e.g., GCS, BP, lactate), airway threat, shock, mechanism, or time course.
  • Does not adjust for age, frailty, anticoagulation/antiplatelets, comorbidity, or competing mortality risk.
  • Does not replace imaging review, specialist interpretation, or evolving clinical reassessment.
  • Embedded examples are pragmatic and intentionally conservative; they may not match local registry coding in edge cases.
  • May under-represent injury burden when multiple moderate injuries occur across more than three regions, because (like canonical ISS) it is dominated by the top three region severities.
Knowledge gap acknowledgement:
There is currently no validated, widely adopted bedside substitute for formal AIS/ISS coding. ISS-Lite is offered transparently as a pragmatic guide, not a surrogate standard.

NMTR and registry alignment statement (scope-limited)

The ISS-Lite calculator is designed to remain conceptually compatible with the six-region ISS/AIS framework used in UK trauma registry workflows (including NMTR and previously TARN), while providing a bedside-friendly interface that separates the spine into cervical/thoracic/lumbar selections for clarity.

Important limitation: “Alignment” here refers to the regional grouping principle and overall ISS-style structure (six regions; top-three squared-sum approach) — not to AIS code assignment, NMTR inclusion decisions, or registry case definitions. ISS-Lite does not perform AIS coding and must not be used for NMTR submission or audit.

Public NMTR materials describe the programme, inclusion framework and data collection scope, and the NMTR data collection standard is published as an update to ISB 1606 (DAPB1606, current catalogue record).
